# Contributing

Contributions are evaluated on traceability, reproducibility, and substantive accepted work — not title, affiliation, attendance, or informal participation.

## Contributor pathway

The default pathway is:

`Applicant -> Participant -> Contributor Candidate -> Validated Contributor -> Maintainer`

- **Applicant:** expresses interest or applies to a Lab/MODERN opportunity.
- **Participant:** joins a workshop, cohort, or defined contribution process.
- **Contributor Candidate:** claims a substantive issue and begins attributable work.
- **Validated Contributor:** completes substantive work that passes review and becomes part of the public research record.
- **Maintainer:** repeatedly demonstrates reliable technical/research judgment and receives stewardship responsibility.

No person becomes a Validated Contributor merely because their name appears on the website, they attend a workshop, join a chat, receive an invitation, or hold an honorary title.

## Founding Researcher Challenge

A qualifying first contribution should do all of the following where applicable:

1. identify one eligible official source or defined research object;
2. add, repair, reproduce, audit, or validate substantive material without guessing missing values;
3. provide complete provenance metadata;
4. document licensing or redistribution constraints when relevant;
5. reproduce or extend at least one validation check;
6. state uncertainty and comparability limitations explicitly;
7. submit the work through an attributable GitHub issue, pull request, audit, or versioned artifact.

## Workflow

1. Open or claim a GitHub issue.
2. Confirm its acceptance criteria before doing substantial work.
3. Work on a branch or fork where code/data changes are required.
4. Keep raw source transcriptions separate from derived outputs.
5. Run validation and tests.
6. Open a pull request or submit the defined audit/artifact explaining the source, change, evidence, and limitations.
7. Respond to review with an auditable record of corrections.
8. Receive accepted/merged status before claiming validated contributor credit.

## What counts as substantive contribution

Examples include:

- a merged data or code pull request meeting the issue's acceptance criteria;
- an accepted independent reproduction report;
- a completed source/provenance audit that changes or validates a release;
- a versioned methodological or annotation protocol accepted into the project;
- a non-trivial validation/test contribution;
- a released workshop or training artifact built to Lab standards;
- a substantial contribution to a non-founder-led dataset/module.

Minor typo fixes, passive attendance, private feedback, website listing, or unreviewed submissions do not by themselves establish contributor status.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Do not fill nulls with zero unless zero is explicitly reported by the source.
- Do not relabel a source period to force cross-country alignment.
- Do not infer missing official observations from news coverage.
- Do not collapse conflict-affected regional series into reassuring aggregate interpretations.
- Do not claim independent verification of your own work.
- Do not represent unfinished, rejected, or unreviewed work as an accepted Lab contribution.

## Credit and role attribution

Accepted substantive work is credited through repository history and release acknowledgements. Releases should describe what each contributor actually did.
